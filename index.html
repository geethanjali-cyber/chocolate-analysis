11 — Final Briefing

Trade Analyst Conclusion

Prepared for the Government Trade Intelligence & Sustainability Division · Distribution: Public

§1 Key Findings

  • Cocoa cultivation remains geographically concentrated, with West Africa supplying the majority of global raw bean volume while capturing a minority of final retail value.
  • Value addition is heavily weighted toward processing and manufacturing economies, widening the gap between origin and destination markets.
  • Transport and packaging together account for a significant share of the product's lifecycle carbon footprint.
  • Traceability remains inconsistent across the chain, complicating enforcement of labour and deforestation standards.

§2 Recommendations

  • Incentivise in-origin processing capacity to shift a greater share of value addition toward producing nations.
  • Expand mandatory supply-chain traceability disclosures aligned with international deforestation-free trade requirements.
  • Support farmer cooperatives with direct trade financing to reduce dependency on volatile intermediary pricing.
  • Standardise mono-material and compostable packaging requirements across major importing markets.

§3 Policy Suggestions

  • Tiered tariff relief for certified sustainable and traceable cocoa shipments.
  • Joint origin–destination cocoa price stabilisation mechanism to reduce farmer income volatility.
  • Cross-border carbon accounting standard specific to agri-food commodity chains.

§4 Future Challenges

  • Climate variability in West African growing regions threatens medium-term yield stability.
  • Rising input costs may accelerate consolidation among smallholder farms.
  • Consumer demand for ethical sourcing is outpacing verifiable certification capacity.
